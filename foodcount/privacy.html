Privacy policy

English · Español · Português · Français

Who we are. Food Count is made by Amg Up, an independent developer. In this policy, "we" means Amg Up. Privacy contact: pedalup180@gmail.com.

Short version. Food Count has no account, no servers, no ads and no analytics of its own. What you log stays on your phone. If you use the barcode scanner, Google receives technical data (see Barcode scanner).

What Food Count stores on your phone. The foods you log, your weight if you enter it, the optional profile (height, weight, year of birth, formula, activity, whether you are pregnant or breastfeeding), your plan and settings, your own foods and recipes, the fasts you record if you turn on the optional fasting timer, and reminder settings. It lives in the private storage of the app. We do not receive it.

What leaves your phone only when you choose. A backup file, a CSV or a PDF that you save or share with the Android menu. You pick where it goes.

Android automatic backup. If Android backup is on for your phone, Android may copy the app files to your Google account, encrypted. This is a setting of your phone and Google, not of Food Count. It never includes the lock, purchases or an unfinished draft.

Purchases. Payments are handled by Google Play. Food Count only learns from Google Play whether Pro is active. The Google Play Billing library talks to Google for that; Food Count does not send your log.

Permissions. Notifications: for the reminders you turn on, and for two notices a few days before your 30 free days end (they say nothing about your food or weight). On Android 13 or newer, Food Count asks for this permission only when you turn reminders on. Restart: to set your reminder again after the phone restarts. Fingerprint: only if you turn on the lock; your phone checks the fingerprint, and Food Count never sees or stores it. Internet and network state: used only by the Google Play libraries for purchases and for the age check described below. Food Count does not ask for location, contacts, microphone or your files, and it does not ask for the camera permission (see Barcode scanner below).

Barcode scanner. To read a barcode, Food Count opens the code scanner of Google Play services, a Google component on your phone. It does not need the camera permission: the camera image stays inside the scanner window of Google, and Food Count never receives it and never saves it. Food Count only gets the number of the code and looks it up in the USDA product list that is inside the app, on your phone. The number is not sent anywhere. Nothing is saved unless you choose to add the food. The first time, Google Play services may download its scanner module (that download is between your phone and Google), and Google may collect technical data about how its scanner works, under its own policies; Food Count does not receive it. When you scan, Google receives, through ML Kit, technical data: device information (maker, model, system version), the app name and version, an installation identifier, and performance and error data. This is collected by Google only while you scan, it is not shared with third parties, and the camera image does not go to Food Count. Product data comes from USDA FoodData Central (Branded Foods, public domain) and reflects the label reported to the USDA: check your package. You can also type the number instead of scanning.

Support numbers. Find support opens your phone dialer with the number typed in. Food Count never calls by itself.

Food data. The food list comes from USDA FoodData Central and is inside the app. No internet is needed to search it. Some foods have a 1-serving size from the FDA reference amounts (21 CFR 101.12), a label reference and not a recommendation of how much to eat. Food Count is not affiliated with or endorsed by the USDA or the FDA.

Adults only. Food Count is for people 18 and older. If the year of birth you enter makes you younger than 18, Food Count saves only that the app is blocked, not your year.

Age information from Google Play. In some places the law asks apps to take care with age. When the app opens and before a purchase, Food Count may ask Google Play for your age range (for example, 18 and older). Google answers only with the range, how it was confirmed, or a note that a parent has not approved; Food Count uses it at that moment to allow purchases or to pause them, and does not save it, send it anywhere or link it to your log. If Google does not answer, or does not share an age, nothing changes.

Deleting your data. Settings > Delete all data erases everything Food Count saved, except the date your free trial started, or uninstall the app. Nothing is kept on a server because nothing is sent to one.

If you live in the European Union or the United Kingdom. The person responsible for this app (the controller) is its developer, Amg Up, at pedalup180@gmail.com. Food Count does not collect or transfer personal data: your log stays on your phone, so you use your rights (access, correction, deletion and portability) directly in the app by exporting, editing or deleting. If you think your rights were not respected, you can complain to the data protection authority of your country.

Changes. If this policy changes, the new version is published here with a new date.
