PassLeap Pilot Terms of Use

Version 1, November 2026. These terms are for the app PassLeap Pilot ("PassLeap: Private Pilot Test"), made by Amg Up ("we").

What PassLeap is

PassLeap is a study aid: practice questions for the FAA written (knowledge) tests for the Private Pilot Airplane and Sport Pilot Airplane certificates, with timed practice tests in the format of the test (60 questions for Private and 40 for Sport, 2 hours each), a figure book with figures from the FAA's public testing supplement, Flight Math (endless flight math with the steps and the name of each mistake), a METAR and TAF decoder, Report codes (a review list made from the codes on your test report), Listen mode (your phone reads the questions aloud with its own voice), the 24 months of 14 CFR 61.39 for your checkride and a readiness score. It is for your own use. PassLeap Pilot is for people 13 and older. You can take the written test from about age 15, and the pilot certificate itself requires age 17 (14 CFR 61.103 and 61.305; student pilot certificate, 16).

PassLeap is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), the U.S. Department of Transportation, any federal or state agency, any testing center, or any flight school. We write our own practice questions from public sources, such as 14 CFR Parts 61 and 91, the Aeronautical Information Manual and FAA handbooks. The figures come from the FAA's public testing supplement (FAA-CT-8080-2H). For official information, visit faa.gov.

This app does not give you a certificate

Only the FAA issues pilot certificates, and only an authorized instructor can give you the endorsement you need for the test. The format, the cost and the rules of the real tests can be different from the practice here.

This app is not for flying

This app is for test practice. Never use PassLeap to navigate or to plan a flight. The charts are test figures from 2018: never use them to fly. The METAR and TAF reports and the flight math problems are made up for practice. Two of the figures are drawings made by PassLeap, and they say so.

Rules change

Rules change: check the date shown in the app and faa.gov. Our questions can have mistakes. Report codes shows the text of the FAA's Airman Certification Standards as the FAA publishes it; your instructor and the FAA have the final word.

Listen mode

Listen mode reads with the voice that is already on your phone and can keep going with the screen off. While it reads, Android shows a notification with Pause and Next. You choose when it starts and you can stop it at any time. Use it only where it is safe to listen.

No promise about your test

PassLeap does not promise that you will pass. Practice tests and the readiness score are estimates from your own practice. The checkride deadline counts 24 calendar months from the month you enter; your test report has the exact date.

The 3 free days

The trial lasts 3 days from the first time you open the app. It does not ask for a card or an account. When it ends you need a plan to take timed practice tests, study every topic, use Flight Math and Listen mode, practice from your Report codes, and use today's study and mistake review again. The figure book, the METAR and TAF decoder, the Report codes list and the checkride deadline stay open, and your progress, your settings and your backups stay available.
