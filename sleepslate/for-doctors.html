SleepSlate for doctors and nurses: how to ask your patients for a sleep diary

A two-week sleep diary is often the first thing you ask for when a patient says they sleep badly. SleepSlate is an Android app that makes that diary easy to keep and easy to read.

What the patient does

Each morning the patient answers 8 short questions (about 1 minute): when they got into bed, turned the lights out, how long it took to fall asleep, how many times and how long they were awake, when they woke up for the last time, when they got out of bed, and how they slept. Naps, caffeine, alcohol, what woke them up and the type of day are optional.

What the PDF shows

Page 1: the averages for the period (time in bed, time asleep, sleep efficiency with its formula written out, time to fall asleep, time awake after falling asleep, awakenings, lights-out and get-up times, quality), then a night-by-night table. The next pages: a 24-hour grid of every night, naps, caffeine and alcohol, what woke the person up, work days and days off, and the notes the patient wrote.

If the patient fills in For my visit, the PDF also lists what bothers them most, how long it has been going on, how it affects their day, what they or a bed partner noticed, and the questions they want to ask you. Everything is reported by the patient, not measured by a device. There are no points, totals or colors: you read the numbers.

How to ask for it

1. Install SleepSlate from Google Play. The first 30 days have everything on; seeing the nights, the PDF, backup and export are always free. 2. Log each morning for 2 weeks; the app can remind them at the time they choose. 3. Put the date of the visit in the app and fill in For my visit. 4. Bring or send you the PDF: it can be shared, printed or saved, on Letter or A4 paper, in English or Spanish.

Privacy

The diary stays on the patient's phone. SleepSlate has no account, no server, no ads and no analytics; nothing is sent to us. The patient chooses when and with whom to share the PDF.

Why these questions

The 8 core questions follow the fields that sleep diaries commonly collect. The questions are written by SleepSlate and are not a copy of any published diary or questionnaire.
